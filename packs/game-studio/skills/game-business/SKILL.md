# Game business analysis

## One question per document
Pick the single question the operator asked. Answer it; do not write a market encyclopedia.

## Structure
1. **Decision** (3 lines): the choice the operator must make, your recommendation, the main risk.
2. **Options**: two or three, each with what it needs, what it costs in team effort, and what could go wrong.
3. **Evidence**: bullets with a source each. Mark estimates "estimate" and unchecked claims "unverified".
4. **Next step**: the smallest experiment that would confirm or kill the recommendation, with the seat that runs it.

## Methods that work for a small team
- Audience: describe two or three concrete player types (what they play now, session length, who they play with). Interview-style questions are better than demographics.
- Positioning: one sentence "like X, but Y", where Y is something this game does that the others do not.
- Monetization: list fair options (cosmetics, supporter packs, optional passes) and the options to avoid. A choice must not change who wins.
- Retention: first-session goal, reason to return tomorrow, reason to invite a friend. Prefer mechanics over notifications.
- Scope: the smallest release that is fun with friends; list what is cut and why.
- Risks: naming and art originality, licences of sounds and fonts, age rating, chat and user content moderation, server cost per concurrent player.

## Honesty
Never present a guess as data. Prefer "unknown, test it by ..." to a made-up figure.
